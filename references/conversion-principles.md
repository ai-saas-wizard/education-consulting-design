# Conversion principles adapted from the supplied guide

The original guide is retained unchanged through Part VIII and its final principles; a clearly separated Part IX adds this project's findings. It is research, not audited conversion-rate evidence. Preserve the useful logic without treating every operator's claims or funnel tactics as proven.

## Design decisions

1. Match the visitor's identity and stage early. A broad education hub may route multiple stages; an application page may have one narrow audience.
2. Explain the mechanism through deliverables, sample work, a lesson preview, or a short process. A named method without component logic adds little.
3. Put relevant proof beside the claim. Prefer baseline, intervention, timeframe, and outcome over unsupported totals. If proof is unavailable, design a real demonstration slot instead of fabricated testimonials.
4. Reduce next-step ambiguity: what the action does, what information it requests, whether it costs money, and what follows it.
5. One visually dominant action per screen does not mean one link on the entire site. Resources, policies, and a quieter diagnostic path can serve different readiness levels.
6. Cold visitors often need explanation before qualification; warm visitors may accept a faster path. Do not infer traffic warmth solely from industry.
7. Video should enhance comprehension. Provide a descriptive poster, visible controls, and equivalent core information in text. Do not make enrollment depend on watching a long video.
8. Show actual delivery: curriculum, feedback cadence, coaching access, tools, expected effort, and duration. Separate immediate access from time-gated bonuses.
9. Qualification should describe fit respectfully. Collect only fields that support the next step. Explain a lengthy application before the click.
10. State known billing and risk terms close to the decision. Do not invent discounts, countdowns, scarcity, guarantees, accreditation, or medical/income outcomes.
11. Judge proposed experiments on qualified applications, booked/showed calls, enrollment, or activation as appropriate. Colors alone do not establish conversion performance.

## Page recipes by path (fallback)

Use these only when there is no `funnel-plan.md`. When a plan exists, its pages and their ordered content decide the sections; map each one to a recipe in the chosen visual system instead. Omit, merge or reorder sections to fit the actual decision. A small offer should not inherit a twenty-section page because a reference has one. Block specifications are in [conversion blocks](conversion-blocks.md).

### VSL-first low-ticket course

**Choose when:** warm social traffic, a price buyers can decide on alone, and a sales video that carries the pitch.

1. **Compact header:** logo and one button to the offer.
2. **Video hero:** qualifier, outcome headline, one-line mechanism, player, CTA with microcopy, statistics strip. The first screen works without playing.
3. **Problem reframe:** why the usual advice fails; the belief shift from what they think now to what's true.
4. **Mechanism:** the named method, three or four components, each in a sentence.
5. **What's inside:** the curriculum as one progression, plus a sample or preview.
6. **Proof:** matched testimonials (problem, what changed, what helped, name or label). Omitted when there is none.
7. **Offer:** tiers with recurrence, inclusions, "best for" and one CTA each; terms under them.
8. **Fit and not a fit.**
9. **Instructor:** only the credibility that matters for this outcome.
10. **FAQ:** access, time, devices, language, payment, refunds, support.
11. **Final CTA:** restate the one decision.
12. **Footer:** legal name, contact, privacy, terms, refund policy, results disclaimer.

### Assessment first

**Choose when:** warm traffic that doesn't know which problem it has, a recommendation that genuinely differs by answer, low or mid ticket.

1. **Compact header:** the button starts the assessment.
2. **Hero, with or without video:** qualifier, outcome headline, what the assessment gives and how long it takes, CTA "start", statistics strip.
3. **Problem reframe.**
4. **Mechanism.**
5. **Assessment entry block:** what they get, time, cost, what data is asked.
6. **What's inside,** briefly.
7. **Proof.**
8. **Offer,** for visitors ready to buy now ("See plans" as the quieter path).
9. **Fit and not a fit.**
10. **FAQ.**
11. **Final CTA** back to the assessment.
12. **Footer.**

Then the assessment flow: intro → one question per screen → contact step → result page (meaning, first steps, how the method helps, recommended tier with the reason, tiers, CTA) → checkout.

### High-ticket application

**Choose when:** the price or fit needs a conversation, capacity is limited, or qualification matters more than volume.

1. **Minimal header:** logo, two or three anchors, "Apply" or "Check my fit".
2. **Hero:** qualifier with stage, outcome headline, mechanism and effort subhead, CTA with what the application involves and the price or threshold; video beside the headline or below the first screen; a proof strip of three to five items.
3. **Problem economics:** what the current way costs them.
4. **Mechanism:** named, with its components explained.
5. **Matched case study:** baseline, constraint, what was done, how long, outcome, caveat.
6. **Deliverables,** grouped by outcome.
7. **How the engagement works:** timeline, cadence, what the client does.
8. **Proof library,** segmented so visitors find someone like them.
9. **Fit and not yet,** with a useful route for people who aren't ready.
10. **Investment and risk:** price or "from", payment options, the real guarantee, real capacity.
11. **FAQ.**
12. **Final CTA:** "Apply", with the number of questions, the time it takes and when they'll hear back.
13. **Footer.**

Then: application → under review or qualified → scheduling → confirmation and preparation → call → enrollment → onboarding, plus the not-yet route.

### Course or membership, product-led

**Choose when:** the product can be shown (lessons, dashboard, community), buyers decide on the page, one-time or recurring.

1. **Header.**
2. **Hero:** outcome and a real product view, CTA, statistics.
3. **Positioning bridge:** the category in a few lines.
4. **Audience fit.**
5. **Fair comparison** with the alternatives.
6. **Inclusions.**
7. **Sample lessons.**
8. **Curriculum.**
9. **Instructor credibility.**
10. **Proof.**
11. **Progression:** what's available now and what unlocks later.
12. **Pricing:** recurrence beside the amount, cancellation terms.
13. **Reviews and FAQ.**
14. **Final CTA.**
15. **Footer.**

### Education hub

**Choose when:** many free resources, visitors at different levels, search traffic; the paid course is one path among several.

1. **Header** with resource navigation.
2. **Educational promise,** with routing by goal or level.
3. **Resource routing:** subjects or paths.
4. **Expert method.**
5. **Course value.**
6. **Learner proof.**
7. **Teacher credibility.**
8. **Primary course action.**
9. **Quieter diagnostic or free-learning path.**
10. **Footer.**

Resource pages: the article, a contextual course CTA, related resources.
